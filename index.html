    FAQ

    Frequently asked questions

    What does an SDR do?

    A sales development representative (SDR) finds and qualifies potential customers, then books meetings for account executives. The day is research, cold calls, emails and social messages. SDRs don't usually close deals. Their job is to start good conversations with the right people at the right time.

    How do I get my first SDR job with no experience?

    Treat the job hunt as prospecting. Pick a short list of companies, research them, and reach out to hiring managers with a short, specific message. Show you can research, write and handle rejection. A mock cold call or a sample email sequence often beats a polished CV.

    How can sales reps use AI?

    Start with an everyday problem, not the tool. Use AI to speed up account research, summarise calls, draft first versions of emails and prepare for objections. Then edit everything yourself. AI won't solve your problems for you. You solve them faster, using AI.

    What is the difference between mentoring and coaching?

    Coaching usually focuses on a specific skill or target over a set period. Mentoring is a longer, two-way relationship: sharing experience, explaining how decisions get made and helping you think through your career. My 1:1s are mentoring, which means honest conversations about the real problems you're facing.

    What makes a good SDR manager?

    A good SDR manager coaches to patterns, not individual bad days. They look past activity counts to what's actually working, keep the CRM honest and give new reps a clear, simple onboarding path. Activity is not progress, and the manager's job is to help reps see the difference.
